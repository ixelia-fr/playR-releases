Which one, for what?

playR

For anyone with games across several stores, several PCs at home, and the wish to find the same library everywhere — plus a TV and a controller in the living room. It is the only one here that works as a server: the library lives on a machine of yours and the others connect to it. It also brings wishlist price comparison, alerts on games being given away and cloud gaming, which none of the others does out of the box. And filing a game — into a list or a status — takes a simple drag and drop, where the others go through a menu.

Pick it if you have several PCs and want the same library everywhere, controller included.

Not for you if you want retro, Linux today, or open source.

Playnite

A highly configurable desktop launcher, with a full-screen controller mode and community extensions. It handles emulators too. In return, almost everything runs through extensions you have to find, install and configure one by one: setting it up is long and tedious, and you have to go back to it with every change. The library itself stays on the machine it is installed on.

Pick it if you play on a single Windows PC and do not mind the time spent configuring.

Not for you if you want it to work without setup, or your library on several PCs.

GOG Galaxy 2.0

GOG’s own client, able to show other platforms through community integrations. Those have not been maintained since 2021, and the software barely moves any more.

Pick it if GOG is your main store and the integrations you need still work.

Not for you if you are counting on new features: the software and its integrations have stalled.

Heroic Games Launcher

An open-source launcher for Epic, GOG and Amazon, which installs and runs those games through Wine and Proton. It handles neither Steam nor the other stores, and its collection management stops at favourites and categories.

Pick it if you play on Linux or Steam Deck.

Not for you if most of your games are on Steam, or you have a large collection to organise.

LaunchBox

Aimed at retro collections: emulators, cover art, skins, and Big Box — paid — for a full-screen controller showcase. On the PC side, it merely imports what is already installed on the machine.

Pick it if your collection is mostly consoles and arcade — ground playR does not cover, and will not.

Not for you if what you really want is to bring your PC stores together.
